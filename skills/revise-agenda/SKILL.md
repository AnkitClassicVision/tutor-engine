# Revise the agenda

Use this step when a homework or an assessment is finished. The agenda is the next slice of work. It is not a new curriculum and it is not a copy of the questions they just did.

## What you read

- Each topic in the check: how many items held, how many missed, and the kind of miss.
- Strength already on the topic: not started, shaky, getting there, solid.
- The same pattern across the last two weeks, not one lucky item.
- A remark if the student or parent left one: what was hard, what they learned, what they would tell a friend. The remark breaks a tie. It does not erase the scores.
- The current lesson plan and any newer school test or parent note. The newer note still wins on what the class is about.

## How the pattern changes the agenda

- Two or more misses on a topic: that topic leads the next homework. The next item shows the weak attempt and the solid one.
- The same kind of miss on several items: name that gap. A repeated step-slip stays on the steps. A repeated missing idea goes back to the idea, not to a harder task.
- A topic that held: keep it on the plan. The next work creates a new example, and the assessment uses a new setting. Do not assign the same sentences.
- A shaky foundation under a hard topic: the agenda adds that one foundation. It does not add the whole chain.
- A strong check, at or above 90 percent, with no repeated miss: the agenda may move one step forward on the plan. It does not skip a topic that is still shaky.
- A weak check, under 70 percent: narrow the agenda to the weakest topic plus the one idea it rests on. Do not pile on new topics.

## What the teacher sees

At the end of homework and at the end of a test, store a note and show it.

- Overt: the obvious hold and the topic that took the misses.
- Quieter: a pattern in what the student actually wrote, such as labels instead of the idea, one phrase used again, or a case that works while creating a new example does not.
- Over time: compare this note with the notes already stored for this class. Name a larger trend only when it shows up again. Otherwise say it is not firm yet.
- So they learn faster: one change to the next homework or test. Do not add a new topic while a trend is still open.

## What you write

One short agenda the next homework and the next assessment both follow.

- Which topics lead.
- What the homework must probe.
- What the assessment must check in a new setting.
- The sentence that must not be reused.

## What you do not do

- Do not throw away a topic that is solid.
- Do not paste the questions from the check into the next set.
- Do not change the math class or the subject list in this step.
- Do not use the student's name.
