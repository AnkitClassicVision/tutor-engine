# Challenge mission

The child already knows the idea well enough to use it. This mission makes the thinking itself the game. Pick the pattern that fits the topic and the request:

| Pattern | Thinking | The story |
|---|---|---|
| Detective case | Analyse | Something odd happened. The child gathers clues, finds the wrong assumption and explains what really happened. |
| Debate panel | Evaluate | Two characters disagree. The child judges their answers against clear criteria and decides who is more right, and why. |
| Builder's workshop | Create | The child designs something that has to work under constraints, tests it in their head, and improves it. |

## The arc (six questions)

1. **Notice** (Bloom 3): a short apply question that sets up the case or the build. Number or written.
2. **Clue** (Bloom 4): analyse a piece of evidence: what changed, what caused it, which part matters.
3. **Fix the mix-up** (Bloom 4 or 5): a friend of Bloop's confidently explains something wrong. Say plainly in the question that the friend has made a mistake. The child names the mistake, explains the right idea and why the friend's idea fails. Use a misconception from the subject map when one is supplied.
4. **Weigh it** (Bloom 5): judge two options, answers or plans against stated criteria. The child chooses and gives reasons.
5. **Make it** (Bloom 6): design, write or plan something new that uses the idea under a constraint.
6. **Defend it** (Bloom 6): change one condition and ask whether their design or decision still works, and why. This is the unfamiliar situation; keep it on ideas the child already has.

Bloom 4 to 6 questions are written answers. Their key starts with `Rubric:` and names what a good answer must contain, so a grader can judge reasoning, not wording. Every question has three short hints that point to evidence and criteria.

## The lesson card

- `hook`: one or two sentences that open the story and make the child want to solve it.
- `steps`: the idea in two or three short lines, as a reminder, not a full lesson.
- `workedExample`: a solved mini-case in the same pattern on a different example.
- `quickCheck`: one quick question with its answer, to warm up.

## Rules

- The story serves the thinking. If the story could be removed without changing what the child has to think about, rewrite it.
- Every question stands on its own: it repeats the facts it needs.
- A mistake made by a character is always labelled as a mistake.
- Use what the child knows. A mission must not need a new prerequisite.
- Reward reasoning: hints point to evidence and criteria, never to the answer.

If an approved subjectMap is supplied, keep its key in mapKey and use its exact title. Select the teaching route from its dimension and observed gap, preserve the success criteria, and use the contrast and misconception fixes for teaching. A worked example must show completed work and reasons, with a meaningful step left in a separate completion problem. Independent questions must use different cases; a paraphrase or equivalent calculation of the teaching example is not fresh evidence. Keep analysis, evaluation and creation demands when requested.
