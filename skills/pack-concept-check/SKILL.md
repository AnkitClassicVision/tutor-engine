# Concept-check pack

The request names a topic. Build understanding, not trivia: every question should be answerable by someone who understands the idea, not only someone who memorised one sentence.

## Balance across a skill

The shares below are for six questions. When the pack asks for more or fewer, keep the same shares: about a third at the starting level, half one step up, and the rest stretch.

1. Two remember/understand checks: a key fact or term (`choice` or `word`).
2. Three understanding checks: explain why or how, sort examples from non-examples, put steps of a process in order, read a described table or diagram.
3. One stretch (`written`): apply the idea to a new situation or compare two things.

Split a broad topic into one to three skills that a teacher would teach separately (e.g. the water cycle → evaporation and condensation; precipitation and collection).

## Writing each question

- Facts must be accurate and uncontroversial at a school level. If something is uncertain or debated, do not ask it.
- `written` answers start with `Rubric:` and list the one or two ideas a good answer must contain, so a short honest answer can earn credit.
- Use `number` only for a real quantity (a date in a timeline, a measured value) and then give `work: null` unless arithmetic is involved.
- Prefer describing a picture in words ("A table shows …") over referring to a picture the learner cannot see.

## Hints

1. Which part of the idea the question is about.
2. A related example or a fact from the lesson.
3. A sentence starter that sets up the answer without giving it.

## Lesson

Hook with a question the learner will be able to answer by the end, the idea in two to four steps, one worked example (a question answered out loud), quick check.

## Good and bad

- Good: "Why does a puddle get smaller on a sunny day?" written, `Rubric: water evaporates; heat from the sun turns liquid water into water vapour`.
- Good: "Which of these is condensation? a) water on a cold glass b) ice melting c) salt dissolving" answer `water on a cold glass`.
- Bad: "What is the water cycle?" with the answer "a cycle of water" (circular, uncheckable).
