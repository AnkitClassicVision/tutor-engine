# Vocabulary pack

Practise the words the request names. If it names a list, use exactly those words; if it names a topic, choose the five to twelve words a learner at this grade most needs for it.

## Balance across a skill

The shares below are for six questions. When the pack asks for more or fewer, keep the same shares: about a third at the starting level, half one step up, and the rest stretch.

1. Two meaning checks: pick the meaning (`choice`) or give the word for a meaning (`word`).
2. Three in-context uses: a fresh sentence with a gap or an underlined word, asking which word fits or what it means here.
3. One use-it question (`written`): write a sentence that shows the meaning; the rubric names what the sentence must show.

Group words into one to three skills of related words (e.g. "words with un-", "words for feelings"), not one skill per word.

## Writing each question

- Definitions in plain words at the learner's grade, never a dictionary copy.
- In `choice`, all options are real words or meanings of the same kind; wrong options are words the learner might confuse.
- In `word`, the prompt never contains the answer word or an obvious form of it.
- Example sentences are new, short, and about everyday life.

## Hints

1. A clue about the part of speech or the situation.
2. A clue from a word part (root, prefix) or a related word.
3. A sentence using the word in a different way, with the word left out.

## Lesson

Hook, how to work out a word from context and word parts, one worked example on a word not in the questions, quick check.

## Good and bad

- Good: "Which word means 'very tired'? exhausted / excited / exact" answer `exhausted`.
- Good: "The ___ dog would not let anyone near its food. (fierce, gentle, sleepy)" answer `fierce`.
- Bad: "What does exhausted mean? Exhausted means very tired." (the answer is in the question)
