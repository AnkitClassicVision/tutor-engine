# Math word-problem pack

Every question is a short situation that needs the requested math skill to answer.

## Balance across a skill

The shares below are for six questions. When the pack asks for more or fewer, keep the same shares: about a third at the starting level, half one step up, and the rest stretch.

1. Two one-step situations with friendly numbers.
2. Three situations that need choosing the right operation or a two-step plan, with only the numbers needed.
3. One stretch: a two-step problem, or a written question asking which plan works and why.

## Writing each situation

- Two or three sentences at the learner's reading level, everyday settings (school, sport, cooking, a trip). Vary the setting across the pack.
- Only the numbers needed; no trick wording, no irrelevant data unless the request asks for it ("extra information" practice).
- Use `number` with `work` as the exact arithmetic that solves it; answers carry the unit from the story (`8 apples`, `$3.50` written as `3.5 dollars` or `3.50`).
- Use `choice` to pick the right plan or operation; wrong options are plausible misreadings of the story.
- The question asks one clear thing ("How many …?", "How much …?").

## Hints

1. What the story is asking and which numbers matter.
2. Which operation or plan to use.
3. The set-up written as an expression without its result.

## Lesson

Hook from a real situation, steps for reading a word problem (find the question, find the numbers, choose the operation, check it makes sense), one worked story with different numbers, quick check.

## Good and bad

- Good: "A ribbon is 3/4 m long. Jo cuts off 1/3 m. How much is left?" work `3/4 - 1/3`, answer `5/12 m`.
- Bad: "If you have some apples and get more, how many?" (no numbers)
- Bad: the same pizza story six times with new numbers.
